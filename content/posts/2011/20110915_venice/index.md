Venice is extraordinary and one of its most stunning assets is Piazza San
Marco. A massive square which contains the Campanile, St. Mark’s Basilica, and
lots of pigeons.

![02](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/02.jpg)

Filled with tourists, this place can seem overwhelming but there is so much
beauty and awe-inspiring architecture in this small area that it truly can
blow your mind.

![03](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/03.jpg)
![04](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/04.jpg)

First and foremost is the Campanile, which at my new home at UC Berkeley lies a
close cousin of this monumental tower. The dazzling brick monolith stands high
above all else in Venice and stands as a musical guardian over the city.

We waited in quite a line  to get into the tower and then crammed in a stylish
elevator for a ride up to the highest point in the city. The view was in no
way disappointing.

![05](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/05.jpg)
![06](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/06.jpg)
![07](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/07.jpg)

It seems like the whole city is within your grasp. Huge expanses of red roofs,
rising cathedral of church towers and twisting streets gripping the sides of
dark waterways. There really is nothing like it. Looking over the giant square
that is the piazza and turning every which way and seeing red roofs for what
feels like miles.

![08](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/08.jpg)

In the Piazza itself was an amusing spectacle. Hundreds of pigeons filling the
square where people where feeding them and letting them crawl all over them.
It was extremely entertaining to watch but I would never let those gross rats
with wings touch me. More photos of this will be seen later.

![09](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/09.jpg)
![10](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/10.jpg)
![11](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/11.jpg)

The Basilica was beyond words. This overly ornate structure is a house of gods
built by thieves. A strange concept to build a holy structure entirely
constructed of goods that were stolen from other places like Constantinople or
any of the other numerous Venetian neighbors. It amounted to quite a
spectacle.

The inside of the church was covered in gold and the most amazing feats of
architecture. It is a strange concept though, if not an hypocritical one to
build a church out of stolen goods. Whether the grounds of which this place
was constructed is correct or not, it still amounted to an intricate and
stunning structure that is nearly indescribable.

![12](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/12.jpg)
![13](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110915_venice/13.jpg)

The Piazza San Marco was an amazing and awe inspiring place, it is still hard to believe that the Venetians were able to squeeze that much beauty into a small square.
