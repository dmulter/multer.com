Today we decided to give Arches a second try but earlier, when there are not
massive hordes of toursists all over the park. We had an extraordinary time!
We first visited the Windows were there were no people at all and had a
pleasant, but extremely fast paced hike as we took pictures of the window
arches.

![01](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/01.jpg)
![02](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/02.jpg)
![03](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/03.jpg)

Afterwards we headed over to the Delicate Arch where we were faced with a
decision. To simply use the overlook of the arch or do a hike up a sheer rock
face to stand right next to the cliff. We almost chose incorrectly and were
thinking about doing the overlook for time’s sake. However in the end the
hiker’s spirit won and we decided to make the trek to the arch. It was a
decision we will never regret.

![04](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/04.jpg)

The pictures do not really give the mountain we climbed credit. It was
difficult, not impossible but very hard in the high altitude. It was a lot of
fun though toughing it out and climbing this mountain. The hike was amazing
with awesome views of the valley and geological structures all along the way.

![05](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/05.jpg)
![06](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/06.jpg)
![07](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/07.jpg)
![08](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/08.jpg)

The climb up the mountain, the views, the amazing pathway, all are small pieces
that made this hike perfect. Ending of course with the famed Delicate Arch.
The most photographed arch in the park, it is clear why so many make this trek
out into the desert; it is so unique and awe-inspiring.

![09](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/09.jpg)
![10](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/10.jpg)

Can you spot me in this next picture?

![11](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/11.jpg)

The cherry on top for this hike was the petroglyphs at the very end.

![12](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/12.jpg)

We had a lot of fun on this hike because it was just so amazing. It is
definitely on the top five if not top two of my favorite hikes list. We were
even able to spot what I believe was a pretty sexy looking mountain goat on
the pathway.

![13](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/13.jpg)

After that amazing experience we headed to the Devil’s Garden to go see the
Landscape arch, possibly the biggest arch in the nation. However after our
amazing hike at the Delicate Arch this one was relatively disappointing.

![14](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/14.jpg)

There were some pretty views from the short hike out but nothing like on the
Delicate Arch.

![15](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/15.jpg)
![16](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/16.jpg)

We had done three hikes and seen the entire park and were thoroughly tired.
However our day had just begun, we still had a long drive and two more parks
to see. Next on the list was a small park called Natural Bridges National
Park.

![17](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/17.jpg)

Comprised of three arches, this park is very small. Sadly we were so tired that
we couldn’t spend a sufficient amount of time necessary to explore this park and
as a result it was slightly disappointing. The arches in comparison to Delicate
Arch were almost shameful.

![18](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/18.jpg)
![19](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/19.jpg)
![20](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/20.jpg)

To exit the park and head to our next destination we had to use the Moki
Dugway, a short stretch of highway with intense switchbacks, incredible
heights overlooking the area near Monument Valley, and did I mention that it
wasn’t paved? Yep, after our flat tire we were extremely tire sensitive and
were pretty afraid to drive on it but we had no choice. It turned out to be
really pretty and very cool.

![21](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/21.jpg)

We decided to make a stop at Goosenecks State Park before we crossed the border
into Arizona to see Monument Valley. The Colorado River has carved out some
amazing bends and canyons into the valleys here which resulted in the creation
of these goosenecks.

![22](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/22.jpg)
![23](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/23.jpg)

Our last park of the day was Monument Valley. However the pictures were not
very good because the valley was so hazy due to the fire down in southern
Arizona.

![24](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/24.jpg)
![25](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/25.jpg)
![26](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2011/20110617_utah/26.jpg)

After that we decided to stay the night in Page, Arizona where we will continue
our journey tomorrow.
