We stayed the night in New Orleans and spent the entire day walking all over
New Orleans to take in the sights of this complex city. There are really so
many facets and corners to this city, I don’t feel anyone could ever fully
explore. Because of this we decided to take a little tour of the city to help
us gather some semblance of what we were doing. But we first decided to go to
the famous and wonderful Cafe Du Monde.

![02](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/02.jpg)
![03](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/03.jpg)
![04](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/04.jpg)

Ask anyone who knows me, they will tell you I do not like coffee, I don’t drink
it but I heard two things about Cafe Du Monde; they have amazing Beignets and
great cafe au lait. So naturally I had to get both, liking coffee or not, I
knew I should try it so I got the frozen cafe au lait and on a hot day made
twice as hot by the sticky humid air, it was like heaven. I can’t even explain
how great it was, all I can say is I am going back tomorrow and if I ever come
back again this will be my first stop. This was also my first ever Beignet and
it was awesome. This little french pillow of dough, deep fried and coated in a
sea of powder sugar, was delectable. This french doughnut was definitely a
good choice, but also a messy one. It was impossible to eat them without
leaving a  trail of powder sugar in your wake.

![05](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/05.jpg)
![06](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/06.jpg)
![07](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/07.jpg)

After that we went on the city and cemetery tour of New Orleans which drove us
around talking about the things outside. I am not going to lie, I didn’t
really like the tour or our guide but it did give a nice break from driving
around, desperately trying to find our way and figure out what to do. So in
some ways it was good and we got to see a lot of the city by bus but
otherwise, I was not a fan. The guide didn’t really seem to know a lot of
anything and kept repeating himself in a bothersome manner. We went to the
Garden District and looked at all the old mansions and houses and then went
through the french quarter. The only thing we did that we otherwise probably
wouldn’t have gotten to do was the St. Louis Cemetery.

![08](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/08.jpg)

We learned all sorts of interesting facts about the burial process in New
Orleans for this cemetery which includes the body being exhumed after a year
and a day and the bones being crushed and then put in a bag to be placed back
in the tomb so it take up less space. Also one tomb can hold up to 150 bodies…
or I guess baggies but that doesn’t sound right at all to put it that way.

![09](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/09.jpg)

After that stop we made a depressing stop at the sight of one of the most
damaged areas by Katrina. The water here reached up to 25 high at one point
and took out almost every single house in the area, only three brick founded
buildings remained, all the others floated away or were destroyed. Pictured
below is the wall for the levy that overflowed leading to this catastrophe. We
also learned that Brad Pitt, ever the hero that he is, decided that someone
needed to help, so he did. He is building 150 houses for those who lost there
homes here and making them large enough to very comfortably house the
displaced families and also powered by solar power to help pay for electric
bills.

![10](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/10.jpg)

However, the most depressing part about this stop was not Katrina itself but
the fact that this tour group took us here to gawk at the despair of the
people who lived and lost their lives here. It felt disgusting to be there in
a huge air conditioned bus sight seeing where so man people lost so much, even
their lives. It was hard for me to stomach that, the only thing that made it
bearable was that thanks to the Brad Pitt Foundation homes many of them seemed
to be doing much better. But even then I only saw a glimpse from a tour bus,
so what could I possibly know.

On a happier note in this neighborhood is a man who apparently makes the best
pralines in New Orleans and he came on our bus from his home and sold us some.
It was my first praline and it was very good and had quite the sugar kick to
it.

![11](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/11.jpg)

After the tour we returned to the French Quarter and decided to tour the city
on our own. We walked all over the place visiting many of the places we had
glimpsed in a drive by moment on the tour.

![12](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/12.jpg)

The first stop was to the French Market, this market which is part flea market
and part food market was really fun and expansive. Spanning several blocks we
wound up and down the isles looking at all that was to offer.

![13](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/13.jpg)
![14](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/14.jpg)

We even found a stand that sold all things alligator including alligator jerky,
gator on a stick, and fresh grilled gator.

![15](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/15.jpg)
![16](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/16.jpg)

We looked through all the jewelry, hats, bags, and most important antiques.

![17](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/17.jpg)
![18](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/18.jpg)

Around the corner from the french market we went and shopped for blocks in
antique and recycled things stores that were just too much fun. We found all
sorts of odd things in this quirky little stores.

![19](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/19.jpg)

The furniture had to have been my favorites, they had tons of old antique
furniture that I would have bought in a heart beat if I didn’t have to lug it
around the country with me. It was really funny weaving in and out of these
shops for what seemed like hours.

![20](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/20.jpg)

Next food stop: muffaletta and more pralines- also known as the best pralines.
First time eating muffaletta which is a meat sandwich with an olive tapenade
like spread which was awesome. Even though I was still full I ate the whole
thing. New Orleans is about food after all isn’t it? Oh and personally I liked
these pralines better…

![21](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/21.jpg)

We wandered around the back streets of the city going into cool shops and
looking at intriguing building and architecture.

![22](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/22.jpg)

One of the types of shops I forced my mom to stop at where Voodoo shops. I have
always found voodoo intriguing and have always wanted to learn more about it.
Having done a bit of research I had heard the place to go was Reverend Zombies
House of Voodoo. This crowded and stuff filled little house was full of little
heads, voodoo dolls and all sorts of creepy things. It was very fun to explore
in and after some prompting from my mom I decided to have my palms read and
future told to me by an odd little woman. It was a strange experience but I
think a good one to have. Still nothing out of this world though 🙂

![23](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/23.jpg)
![24](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/24.jpg)

We also stumbled upon the Faulkner house, where William Faulkner lived and
wrote several of his books. This cute little place is now a book store and was
fun to poke around in for a short minute.

![25](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/25.jpg)
![26](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/26.jpg)

Oh, and we met this guy, and yes that is a washboard he has on and is playing.
We found him in the street, no literally, in the street in front of traffic,
playing his washboard.

![27](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/27.jpg)

Our last real stop of the day besides dinner was Preservation Hall. I was
really surprised about this because I had imagined it very differently. I
didn’t imagine this beat up washed out place that didn’t really even have a
noticeable sign that was right across from the voodoo shop. I don’t know what I
was expecting but this sue wasn’t it. And it was closed to boot.

![28](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/28.jpg)
![29](https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/multer.com/content/posts/2012/20120624_day6/29.jpg)

We had dinner at Mother’s Restaurant a local famous place that we heard a lot of
hype about, especially for their Po’ boys. Po’ boys are sandwiches that got
their name because originally they were the poor man’s sandwich and cooks
would hand out this sandwiches to the poor boys. I got a fried shrimp po’ boy
and my mom got some nice gumbo. The food was good but I don’t think it stood
up to the hype at all. Good but not anything to really rave about in the end.
It was still nice to end the day and our time in New Orleans with a New
Orleans’ classic like the Po’ boy .

I really enjoyed my time here in New Orleans and hope to be able to come back
because I think there is so much left to do here. It also seems as if we are
bing chased out of New Orleans by a possible hurricane, so maybe it is time to
head out of town.  I am sad to go but the trip must go on, tomorrow we are
going to Nashville. This is the big change in the trip, no longer are we going
eastward, we are in the final leg heading north now. Half way done.
